Shadow and Silence - Sam Ames' Thirst for Validation from Fake Internet Points

October 9, 2023 - 4 mins read

A Look Through the Filtered Lens

She waxed lyrical about an upcoming photoshoot, and alarm bells rang faint but clear in my head. There’s a breed of guys clutching secondhand Canon EOS cameras who fancy themselves the next Annie Leibovitz. They spin yarns about artistic enlightenment to coax women into “just-for-fun” photoshoots at home—camouflaged come-ons, if ever there were. When I voiced my qualms, she pacified me with assurances that she’d call the whole thing off.

In my head, the scene played out: she’d been there, following the so-called photographer’s prompts, later uploading the snaps to Instagram—those images, however, were cloaked from my eyes. Instagram had a “bug” then, a tell-tale sign of a quasi-shadow-ban lay in the mismatch between the number of posts displayed and the account’s total post count.

It was not digital sleuthing which led to the jarring discovery. Public profiles spill their secrets to anonymous browsers, and, after reinstalling the operating system on my laptop I had an untouched newly installed browser instance, too. At the time I was still in the habit of browsing social media sites without any real purpose. I stumbled upon her profile. The sight of those photos felt like a gut punch—Realizing that I was not signed into Instagram I entered my credentials so that I could participate in the conversation taking place around these images. Images I’d been led to believe did not exist. I signed in only to observe a startling coincidence - the timing was odd - she must’ve yanked the photos down just as I had noticed them only a moment ago. The brutal truth was that I’d been barred from viewing.

I grappled with the fallout, a cocktail of visceral sickness and emotional anarchy. My trust in my own judgment cratered; I chastised myself for the naivete of my exposed underbelly. In hindsight, it seemed I was served just deserts for the crime of seeking a loyal partner in the Digital Age.

That ache lodged itself in me, persistently. Throughout the weeks that followed I brought up what I’d found. This yielded no acknowledgment, no olive branch, and no apology. Not even recognition that my feelings were at all valid. In fact, she would argue at length that my I was not angry, not sad, not hurting, and had not been misled. There was no solace for me. The gang had reached the consensus that painted Sam’s actions as rightful artistic liberty.

To twist the knife, Sam later informed me that her dad had expressed a longing for her to find someone for whom “that sorta thing was okay.”

In the context of Sam’s secret photo-shoot, the “Gee-I-sure-do-wish-that-sorta-thing-was-okay” worldview was just another chapter in her dad’s anthology of indulgence, a relentless affirmation that whatever Sam did was beyond reproach, no matter the burns suffered by others. His influence was a subtle poison, and his pearls of “wisdom” left her ill-equipped for empathy in our narrative. Sam’s relational blueprint, sketched by her dad’s hand, never included the rigors of critical thought. With her yes-man, spine-deficient dad preferring friendship over fatherhood, it’s no wonder she treated me more as a foe than a confidant.

For years, I asked Sam for her dad’s mobile number or for her to share mine with him. Given his insidious presence in my relationship with her, and now with my son (despite this man not being kin to my boy), I thought it wise to build up a rapport with him. I hoped to earn a chance at being considered worthy of basic human dignity in what Sam would reveal were many adversarial conversations about our relationship that included my name but in which I had no voice. This was by design, Sam adamantly refused to make that connection happen. Any time I brought up the subject she’d refuse. You see, contact between he and I might temper the vitriolic tone of those conversations Sam on which Sam had grown overly reliant. Moreover, the bizarre hero fantasy that Sam enables for this man might also be negatively affected in the light of the Sun.
