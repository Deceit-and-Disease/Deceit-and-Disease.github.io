# Welcome to "The Sam Show"  
  
**_Imagine trying to lead a symphony of deaf musicians – that was akin to navigating Sam’s smoking saga. My pleas, crescendoing into a cacophony, prompted me to pen an email, a written appeal for her to quit smoking, crafted in the hope she’d absorb its urgency at her leisure. Yet, this email morphed into the family’s gossip du jour, branding me a "Narc."_**  
  
Pause for a moment. Some mothers do everything by the book, flawlessly, yet life still throws curveballs at them and their children. It’s a harsh truth: bad things can happen to good people without rhyme or reason. With Sam’s smoking, we faced an avoidable risk, a rare instance in parenting where there’s a clear right and wrong choice. Yet, my plea for my son’s mother to quit smoking during her pregnancy earned me nothing but scorn from the Ames family, who mockingly dubbed me a "Narc.”  
  
## The Disservice Doula  
  
During our very first encounter, one of Sam’s sisters insisted that I receive her ‘wisdom.’ She was mad as hell, and was going to let me have it, set me straight. She adamantly professed that smoking during pregnancy was harmless, even suggesting it reduced the risk of pre-eclampsia. She delivered this misinformation with the kind of confidence you’d expect from a seasoned healthcare professional, despite being as medically qualified as Dr. Pepper is an M.D.  
    
## Broken Promises  
    
Sam can lie to world but she cannot lie to me; I was front and center watching as she smoked the _entire_ pregnancy. The whole ten months. She even promised to quit a few times. When those promises went unfulfilled t was a fight I had to pick-I couldn't help myself. The thing is that you cannot reason with impulsive people, loyal only to their immediate desires. Add to this that a person who casually dismisses the validity of any human experience at odds with their worldview, incapable of love, has no interest in facts-they operate solely on their latest impulses. I was scarcely more than an extra, a bit player in the The Sam Show. The more certain I grew that I wasn’t being acknowledged or heard the louder my volume. Months later the secondhand smoke of her addiction lingered, and Sam pinned her relentless habit on me, alleging that my constant need to take issue with her bad decision only heightened her need to do it.  
  
> **_“If you had been cooler about it I would’ve quit."_**  
  
In her skewed version of events, my genuine concern had the opposite effect, exacerbating her smoking – a blatant shirking of responsibility that left me astounded. How does one navigate through a ~~pathological clusterfuck~~ morass of irrationality where logic and accountability are the first victims?
