#  Gone  
  
_The signs were unmistakable. Within a month, our once frequent, daily interactions dwindled to sporadic and strained exchanges, leaving me to vie for the privilege of a FaceTime glimpse of my son. Her reluctance was palpable, replacing our routine calls and texts with silence or annoyance._

> **Heads Up:** If you've been victimized by a partner's deceit and then further by the gaslighting that ensued, I acknowledge you. You understand the unfolding script. Your past intuitions were valid—they still are. There's no twist of redemption, no moment of vindication ahead. If reliving such experiences is too much, I understand if you choose to step away now.  

I saw the end coming, recognizing the shape of her deceit. Her "break from social media" was nothing more than a thinly veiled ruse, a feeble attempt to create a smokescreen for her dishonesty. She craved the freedom to stray without the specter of accountability. Geographic distance assured her of my physical absence, and my severed ties with mutual acquaintances eliminated any risk of unwanted revelations. A short-lived affair would see her return from her digital sabbatical; a more promising entanglement might prolong it indefinitely.  
  
I clung naively to the hope she'd shield me from humiliation and preserve my connection with our son, even as she wandered. But such an effort—or honesty—would limit her options. Instead, she severed my ties to my son, first convincing herself the warning of her decreased phone time was sufficient to justify her actions. Later, when I stopped chasing the disappointment that came with being marginalized she would say that it was I who left.

Her potential for emotional neglect was not lost on me. I understood the trajectory of our narrative and braced myself for the impact, yet foresight did little to soften the blow.  
  
Each day began the same. In the chill and solitude of dawn, a familiar burning in my nose foretold the tears that would come. Alone, I had no need to hide my grief, yet I longed for escape from the weight of these emotions. The video calls that once anchored my mornings, the sporadic messages that eased my sense of isolation—they had been usurped by a pair of losers, bums. I found myself adrift.  
  
Depression was an old adversary, but this was a new beast, shrouded in a dense fog of despair. Hope, it seemed, was a necessary delusion, a mantra that played endlessly in my psyche—"I just want to go home." A nonsensical chant, as 'home' was now but a haunting memory on constant replay.  
  
By night, I wandered the house, replaying passionate yet futile arguments for a love that had turned one-sided. My mind's theater cycled through confrontations, readings to my son, and imagined moments of unity. I crafted an idyllic past that never was, seeking peace in a void.  
  
Five months in, reality pierced the fog: here I was, in the most stable and nurturing environment I'd ever known, yet ensnared in a battle with a darkness so profound it threatened to engulf me. But, there's no tale of resurgence here, no phoenix rising. Work became my refuge, the only space where I felt a semblance of worth. Despite external affirmations of my professional ascent, each day was an odyssey through emotional quicksand.  
  
Recently, the familiar refrain surfaced again—"I just want to go home." With hindsight's clarity, the phrase now held new meaning, a longing for something beyond the tangible.  
  
My journey through faith provided a rich cultural tapestry and a foundation for my ethical framework. I once believed in an afterlife's promise of redemption. Though now, that belief has receded into the ether of my former self, the allegories still tint my bleakest moments—a yearning for a home that might never have been.
